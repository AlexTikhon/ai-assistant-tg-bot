# Backyard Astronomy Notes

My observing notes from the garden, collected over the winter.

## The inner planets

Mercury is the smallest planet in the solar system and the closest to the sun. It is hard to spot because it never strays far from the sun, so the best time to look for it is shortly after sunset or before sunrise in spring and autumn. A year on Mercury lasts only 88 Earth days, but one day lasts about 59 Earth days.

Venus is the brightest planet in the evening sky and can even cast a faint shadow. Through a small telescope it shows phases like the moon.

## Equipment

I use a 130 mm reflector on a simple mount, a red flashlight to protect my night vision and a printed star chart. A planisphere is cheaper than any app and never runs out of battery. Warm clothes matter more than a bigger telescope.

## Observing log

On the clearest night in February I could see the four brightest moons of Jupiter and the bands on the planet itself. Saturn will be well placed in the autumn. Seeing is best when the air is still, usually after midnight and away from the town lights.
